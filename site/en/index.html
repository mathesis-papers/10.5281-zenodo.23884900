Counterexamples to Wang's Conjectures 3.26 and 5.3 on semigroup algebras

Haobo Ma, Wenlin Zhang

Paper

Can the way formal expressions cancel control how many terms a sum can contain when none can be removed without changing it? In counterexamples to two conjectures in G. Wang’s 2025 manuscript, Haobo Ma and Wenlin Zhang keep an algebraic measure fixed at one while the proposed measures of indispensable terms grow without limit.

Their construction pairs each finite subset of a set U with a level: zero, one or two. Addition takes the union of the subsets and adds the levels, stopping at two; the empty subset at level zero is the identity. Repeated addition stabilizes, making this a periodic commutative monoid. An element equal to its own double is called idempotent. Levels zero and two are idempotent; a level-one element doubles to its level-two counterpart. Its two-element positive cyclic semigroup has no identity, hence is not a group. The other levels generate singleton groups.

A sequence is irredundant if no proper subsequence has the same sum; the empty subsequence has sum equal to the identity. The relative Davenport constant D₍A,k₎ measures the greatest irredundant length with sum consisting of subset A at level k. The authors prove D₍A,k₎ = |A| + k. Separate zero-level singleton subsets and k empty-subset level-one terms attain this length. For example, a singleton at level one needs both its zero-level singleton term and an empty-subset level-one term. For the upper bound, reserve at most k terms supplying the required level. Every remaining term must contribute a point absent from every other term, or it could be deleted. There are at most |A| such terms.

The algebraic measure works differently. Over a nonzero commutative ring with identity, the ordinary semigroup algebra has an independent basis symbol for every element; multiplication follows monoid addition. Even an absorbing element remains a basis label, not algebraic zero. For each sequence term, subtract from its symbol a nonzero scalar times the symbol of its eventual idempotent. The algebraic Davenport constant d is the supremum of lengths for which the product of these differences is nonzero for every choice of nonzero scalars.

The authors obtain d = 1 over every such ring, including rings with zero divisors. For any sequence of length at least two, choose all scalars equal to one. An idempotent term gives a zero factor. Otherwise, the first two terms have level one: multiplying their differences produces four identical basis labels, all with the same union and level two, whose signs cancel. Conversely, the empty subset at level one and its level-two idempotent have distinct basis labels. Their single factor cannot vanish, whatever nonzero scalar is chosen.

Wang’s first conjecture identifies elements generating the same principal ideal, meaning the same set reachable by further addition, and asks for bounded relative constants in this Green quotient when d is finite. Only targets whose positive cyclic semigroups are not groups are considered. Here distinct elements have distinct principal ideals, so the quotient changes nothing. The associated Schützenberger groups describe permutations within these equivalence classes; singleton classes make them trivial. With U the natural numbers, this gives one infinite monoid with d = 1 but relative constants m + 1 at m-point subsets of level one, contradicting the conjectured bound.

Taking U with m points gives finite monoids of size 3·2ᵐ. Wang’s second conjecture proposes an equality combining nongroup relative constants with algebraic constants of those associated groups. Here the group contributions vanish, giving the proposed value m + 1 instead of the actual value one. Every field meets Wang’s splitting-field hypothesis because every cyclic period equals one. Already m = 1 gives six elements and one versus two. Wang studied relative constants in 2017; Smertnig previously found algebraic–combinatorial discrepancies for finite groups, with algebraic constants exceeding the corresponding combinatorial bound. Here all associated groups are trivial.

These are general written proofs, not merely finite computations. The manuscript credits the trureturing contributors with a Lean declaration refuting the infinite boundedness conjecture over the rationals using d ≤ 1; it does not claim that declaration establishes the arbitrary-ring equality or finite counterexamples. The example also contradicts a companion remark interpreted for semigroups, not its literal wording about groups. The significance is that immediate algebraic cancellation can coexist with arbitrarily many indispensable subset points. The note disproves the proposed general controls without supplying a replacement characterization of when they hold.
